## Draft comment for #95

> The registry has `gen_ai.tool.call.arguments`, `call.id`, `call.result`,
> `definitions`, `description`, `name` and `type`, and no attribute that names a
> human, an approval, an authorization or a policy. This issue has named that
> slot since April and the thread has done most of the design work. Here is a
> model that implements what the thread converged on, so there is something
> concrete to argue with.
>
> Seven attributes on `execute_tool` and the MCP `tools/call` spans, all opt-in,
> all payload-free: an authorization status enum, an evidence grade, and opaque
> digest/format/locator references for the evidence, the action and the policy.
>
> What it takes from this thread rather than inventing:
>
> - Approval is a pre-execution control state, not a tool result (@rpelevin).
>   The status describes the decision; the span's existing result and
>   `error.type` still describe the execution.
> - A denied call leaves a decision behind and no successful child execute span
>   for the same call id (@AgentGymLeader). That is normative in the model.
> - No raw approver identity, and in fact no approver attribute at all
>   (@AgentGymLeader, @rpelevin). We dropped the approver reference we had
>   originally sketched after reading those two comments; the evidence locator
>   already carries whatever the producer wants to resolve out of band, and an
>   approver field only adds a re-identification surface to a span that leaves
>   the producing system.
> - Telemetry observes the decision and is not the authority (@Rul1an). Stated
>   in the model's own note, not just in a PR description.
>
> The one attribute that is not from this thread is `evidence.grade`
> (`self_attested` / `third_party_logged` / `independently_verifiable`), and it
> is the one worth arguing about. Without it every status looks equally
> reliable: a boolean somebody flipped and a signature over a digest of this
> exact action land in the same column. With it, a self-attested status looks
> self-attested to whoever is reading the dashboard, which is what makes the
> field safe to accept without importing anyone's trust model. It is a producer
> claim; nothing in the pipeline verifies it, and the model says so.
>
> On the three digest attributes: I have read #239's non-goal wording, which
> lists "evidence hashes" among the things that should live behind an opaque
> ref, and I want to put the disagreement on the record rather than route
> around it. #239's stated concern is payloads and PII, and its own examples of
> what to exclude are JSON, prompts, tool arguments, policy bodies and user
> data. A digest is the opposite of a payload: fixed length, no plaintext, and
> it is the only thing that makes a handle substitution-resistant. A locator
> alone tells you where to look; a locator plus a digest tells you whether what
> you found is what the call was decided on. If the SIG reads the exclusion as
> covering digests as such, three of these seven attributes should be dropped
> and the remaining four still do useful work.
>
> Composition with the neighbours, briefly: `gen_ai.tool.risk.level` (#373) is
> the assessed input, this is the observed outcome, and together they answer
> "which irreversible tool calls ran with no human step". #159's
> `pause.reason = human_input` says the agent waited; this says what the human
> decided. #132's `action = ask` is the detection-time enforcement decision;
> this is the final outcome of the step it triggered. #239 is the general
> decision-point shape and this is its tool-call specialization, sharing the
> same opaque handle where a producer emits both. If #132/#165 moves control
> surfaces to `agent.*`, this group should move with them.
>
> Reference implementation and worked examples, Apache-2.0:
> `standards/staged/otel-genai-authorization/` in
> `emiliaprotocol/emilia-protocol`, with the group emitted from four agent
> adapters through `@opentelemetry/api` only, under a vendor prefix until this
> lands. The mapping rule those adapters follow is the one I would like the
> convention to state: do not report a value the runtime cannot support. A
> boolean approval slot supports `auto_approved` and `rejected`, and does not
> support `authorized_in_scope`, because a boolean never carried a scope.
>
> One gap I have not resolved and would rather the SIG decide: @JM-Lab's
> `ask-failed` case. `rejected` currently covers both a human decline and a
> fail-closed refusal after the approval interaction itself broke, and those are
> operationally different. That is either a tenth status value or a separate
> reason attribute, and I do not have a view worth defending.

## What this contribution is not

- It is not an Internet-Draft and does not become one. No new `-00`.
- It is not a registry we host. The plate is OpenTelemetry's; we are filling a
  slot in it.
- It is not an evidence product. The attributes are references to evidence that
  lives somewhere else, in whatever format the producer already has.
- It does not require EMILIA. A producer with a boolean and nothing else emits
  two attributes and is done. That is the point: the cost of supply is one
  attribute map, and the vocabulary travels whether or not anyone buys anything.

## The honest limit of the compelled-reader argument

An observability backend indexes the span attributes it is sent. That is a
compelled READ, and it is the whole of the compulsion. Nobody is compelled to
VERIFY anything. `evidence.grade` exists so that the absence of verification is
visible in the data rather than hidden by it, and so a grade can rise from
`self_attested` to `independently_verifiable` later without a schema change. It
is not a claim that anyone will demand the higher grade.

Do not describe this work as making authorization verifiable. It makes the
absence of authorization queryable. Those are different sentences and only the
second one is true today.
